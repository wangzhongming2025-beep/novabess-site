What a portable home battery backup actually is

A portable backup is a self-contained, wheeled power station. Inside is a LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) battery, an inverter and outlets. You charge it from a normal socket (or solar panels, or a generator), then plug appliances straight into it — either through its own AC sockets or an extension lead. There is no connection to your home's main panel.

That single fact is what separates it from a fixed no-solar battery or a rooftop-backed system: those must be wired into the distribution board by a licensed electrician. A portable unit is furniture, not infrastructure.

Why "no install" is the whole point

  • Renters: You cannot touch the building's wiring. A plug-in unit needs no approval and goes with you when you move.
  • Apartment dwellers: Shared panels and body-corporate rules make fixed storage impractical. A wheeled unit lives inside your unit.
  • Move-out & flexibility: Take it to the next place, the cabin, the campsite — it isn't bolted to a wall.
  • No permit, no inspector: Because nothing is permanently connected, there is no electrical permit or utility paperwork.
  • Disaster prep on a deadline: Storm season is here — a portable unit is useful the afternoon it's delivered, not three weeks after an installer visit.

Frequently asked questions

What is a portable home battery backup?

A portable home battery backup is a wheeled, self-contained LiFePO4 power station you plug appliances into directly — via its own AC outlets or an extension lead. Unlike a fixed wall or stack battery, it is not wired into your home's distribution panel, so it needs no electrician, no permit and no landlord approval. NovaBESS PowerBox ships in 4.02 / 8.04 / 16.08 kWh and charges from the wall, solar panels or a generator.

Can renters use a home battery backup?

Yes — a portable, plug-in unit is the only realistic option for renters, because you cannot modify the apartment's wiring or mount equipment on a shared wall. You simply place it inside, charge it from a standard socket, and run essentials (fridge, lights, router, medical devices) off its outlets. That is exactly the use case covered in our apartment renter guide.

How big a portable backup do I need for a blackout?

For a fridge, lights, router and phone through an overnight outage, a 4 kWh unit (PowerBox 4.02 kWh, 500 W) typically runs those loads 12-30+ hours. Add a sump pump or small AC and step up to 8 kWh (1,000 W) or 16 kWh (1,500 W). Use our Home Battery Calculator to size by your exact watts and hours.

Is a portable battery safer or cheaper than a generator?

For indoor or apartment use a portable battery is far safer — no fuel, no exhaust, no noise, no refuelling. Up front it costs more per kWh than a petrol generator, but it needs no fuel, no maintenance and runs silently indoors. A generator still wins only for days-long, whole-house loads outdoors.

Do I need solar for a portable backup battery?

No. PowerBox charges from any wall socket, so it is ready the day it arrives. Adding solar panels simply lets it recharge for free during a multi-day outage or off-grid stay — handy, but optional. This is the key difference from a fixed rooftop system: the battery is useful with zero installation.
