Plug-In Battery vs Portable Power Station: No-Install Backup Explained

No InstallPlug-InRentersPowerBox

A new wave of home batteries needs no electrician. You order one online, plug it into a wall socket, and it starts saving you money and covering blackouts. But "plug-in battery" and "portable power station" are not the same thing — and the difference decides which one fits your home. Here's the honest breakdown, who each is for, and why the NovaBESS PowerBox (a portable, device-plug unit) is the pragmatic pick for renters and apartments.

Bottom line: A plug-in battery feeds your home's circuits through the wall socket (whole-circuit backup, ~800 W typical). A portable power station (like PowerBox) is a wheeled battery you plug appliances into directly (device-by-device backup, 500–1,500 W). Both need zero installation. PowerBox ships in 4 / 8 / 16 kWh, charges from wall, solar or generator, and is CE / UN38.3 / UL / IEC certified.

Why "no install" is the whole point

  • No electrician, no permit. A fixed 5–10 kWh wall battery costs €5,000–€9,000 installed — much of that is inverter, meter-cupboard work and labor. A no-install unit skips all of it.
  • You can move it. Renters and frequent movers take it to the next place. Owners park it where needed.
  • Renter-friendly. No wiring changes, no landlord approval, no lease breach.
  • Works without solar. Charge from the grid at off-peak rates and discharge when prices spike — the same arbitrage logic behind our peak-shaving guide.

Where the plug-in category is exploding

This is not a niche forever. The "plug-in home battery" is a fast-growing European category:

  • Germany — invented the category; balcony and socket batteries are mainstream.
  • Netherlands — turned it into a mass-market phenomenon on dynamic tariffs.
  • UK — opening up fast (Octopus-style dynamic pricing rewards cheap-charge / peak-discharge).

If you sell or install in these markets, a no-install battery is now a top entry product — and a natural add-on to the fixed systems we cover in our peak-shaving country guides (Australia, UK).

Frequently asked questions

Which should I choose — plug-in or portable?

If your home allows socket-feeding and you want whole-circuit backup automatically, a plug-in battery fits. If you rent, want to move it, or prefer to power devices one by one, a portable power station (PowerBox) is the pragmatic choice. Both need zero installation.

Can a no-install battery run a refrigerator?

Yes. A plug-in unit covers a fridge through the socket; a portable station runs it off its AC outlet. Size to the compressor's running and start watts — our apartment guide walks through it.

Is a no-install battery cheaper than a fixed system?

Usually 3–4x lower cost per kWh all-in, because you skip the inverter, meter work and installer labor. You trade some capacity and whole-house coverage for simplicity and mobility.
