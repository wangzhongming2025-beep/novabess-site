When the grid goes down in a storm, heatwave or wildfire, you don't have time to book an electrician. A portable, plug-in battery is useful the afternoon it's delivered — charge it from the wall, roll it where you need it, and keep the things that matter on. No wiring, no fuel, no noise. Here's how to build a no-install blackout kit.

Bottom line: The fastest blackout protection is a wheeled LiFePO4 power station — no electrician, ready same-day. NovaBESS PowerBox (4 / 8 / 16 kWh, CE / UL) keeps medical devices, the fridge and comms on silently and fume-free, and recharges from solar during extended outages.

Why no-install matters during an outage

By the time the lights go out, a fixed battery is too late — it needs an electrician, a permit and often a panel upgrade you can't schedule mid-storm. A portable unit skips all of that:

  • Same-day ready: Charge from any socket; useful within hours of delivery.
  • No tradesperson: No electrician, no inspection, no waitlist.
  • Roll it to the need: Kitchen for the fridge, bedroom for a CPAP, hallway for lights.
  • Take it anywhere: The cabin, the relative's house, the shelter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best no-install backup for a power outage?

A wheeled, plug-in LiFePO4 power station is the best no-install option for most homes. It needs no electrician and is ready the day it arrives — charge it from any wall socket, then run fridge, lights, router and medical devices off its outlets. NovaBESS PowerBox ships in 4 / 8 / 16 kWh (500 / 1000 / 1500 W) and is CE / UL certified.

Portable battery or generator for a blackout?

For indoor or near-home use, a battery wins: silent, fume-free, no fuel to store or queue for, and safe to run overnight. A generator is louder, emits CO, needs fuel and can't run indoors. A generator only beats a battery for days-long, whole-house loads outdoors — and even then, many households pair both.

How long will a portable battery last in an outage?

A 4 kWh unit runs fridge + lights + router + phone for 12-30+ hours; 8 kWh roughly doubles that; 16 kWh covers multi-day essentials and a small AC. Add solar panels and it recharges for free during extended outages. Size exactly with the Home Battery Calculator.

Can a portable battery run a medical device during an outage?

Yes — CPAP machines, oxygen concentrators and similar low-watt medical devices (typically 40-150 W) run comfortably on a portable LiFePO4 unit for many hours. Always keep the device's own backup battery as a final fallback, and confirm the unit's pure-sine-wave output matches the device before relying on it.
