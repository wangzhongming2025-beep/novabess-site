Home Battery Peak Shaving in the UK 2026: Octopus Go, Agile & How Much You Save

United KingdomOctopusArbitrageSmart Meter

The UK has the most developed time-of-use electricity market in Europe. Octopus Energy alone runs millions of homes on tariffs that price power by the hour — and a home battery is the simplest way to exploit that spread. A 10 kWh battery on Octopus Go earns roughly £770-870 a year doing nothing but charging cheap overnight and discharging into the expensive evening peak. No solar required.

Bottom line: On Octopus Go, a 10 kWh usable battery cycling once a day saves about £773/year (charge 9.5p, discharge ~33p, 90% efficiency). A comparable 11.5 kWh battery publishes ~£870/year. At UK installed prices (~£6,000-9,000 for 10 kWh in 2026) that is a payback of 5-8 years, with 4-10 years of pure saving afterwards. Solar is optional — arbitrage alone pays the battery back.

Why the UK is Europe's arbitrage sweet spot

Two forces converged. First, dynamic and time-of-use tariffs went mainstream: Octopus Go, Agile, Flux and Intelligent Go now serve a huge customer base, and Tibber brings Nordic-style hourly pricing. Second, the spread between off-peak and peak is wide and predictable — overnight power is a fraction of the 4-9 pm peak. A battery is a set-and-forget machine that buys low and uses its own stored power when prices spike.

Payback in 2026

A 10 kWh home battery in the UK installs for roughly £6,000-9,000 in 2026. Against ~£773/year of arbitrage saving, that is a payback of about 5-8 years; faster on Agile in a good year, slower on Economy 7. After payback, every year of the battery's 10-15 year life is pure saving — potentially £3,500-8,500+ net over the lifetime. Solar pushes both the saving and the payback further in your favour, but it is not the thing that makes the maths work.

What you need (and the one spec that matters)

  • A smart meter so the supplier can bill half-hourly and you can see the spread. Required for Go/Agile/Flux.
  • A battery with an EMS or hybrid inverter that supports scheduled AC grid charging in TOU mode — this is the single point of failure. Without it the battery cannot actually fill from the cheap overnight window on a timer and the saving collapses.
  • A TOU or dynamic tariff (Octopus, Tibber, EDF, E.ON, OVO all run one). On a flat tariff there is no spread to capture.
  • Right-sized capacity: 7-12 kWh usable fits most homes; 12-18 kWh if you also run an EV and heat pump. The Smart Export Guarantee (SEG) pays only 3-6p/kWh for export, so storing and self-using is worth far more than exporting.

Frequently asked questions

Is battery arbitrage worth it in the UK?

Yes, if you are on a time-of-use tariff. The UK has the most developed dynamic-tariff market in Europe — Octopus alone serves millions of households on Go, Agile and Flux. A 10 kWh battery on Octopus Go earns roughly £770-870/year in pure arbitrage (charging cheap overnight, discharging into the evening peak). No solar is needed; solar only adds free daytime charging on top.

What is the best UK tariff for battery arbitrage?

For most households Octopus Go is the best balance: a fixed ~9.5p/kWh off-peak window (00:30-05:30) against a ~33p daytime rate — about a 23p spread. Intelligent Octopus Go extends the window to 6 hours at 8p (needs an EV). Octopus Agile is the highest-potential but most volatile: half-hourly wholesale prices that can hit 5p overnight or 50p at peak, occasionally negative. Economy 7 is widely available but its ~14p off-peak rate gives smaller savings (~£350-450/yr).

How much can a home battery save on Octopus Go?

On a 10 kWh usable battery, one daily cycle: charge at 9.5p, discharge at ~33p, times 90% efficiency = about £2.12/day, or ~£773/year. Published installer figures for a comparable 11.5 kWh battery run ~£870/year. The saving scales with battery size and the exact regional peak rate (32-36p across the UK).

Do I need solar for battery arbitrage in the UK?

No. Arbitrage works on grid charging alone — the battery fills from cheap overnight power and discharges into the expensive evening peak. That spread pays the battery back on its own. Solar adds a second free charge source and lifts savings further (a 4kWp array plus battery can save £1,000-1,200/year), but it is not required to start. You can always add panels later.
